Recall

Visit the siteSample data

Recall is a memory archive for one person living with dementia. Her photographs and films are grouped into numbered channels, and she opens one by placing a card on a reader in her living room, or by typing its two-digit number. Her family adds the memories from their own phones and laptops.

Overview

Her screen, and her family’s workspace.

Her half is a TV or PC in the living room, always on, read from the sofa several metres away. She has a card reader and a remote with numbers, arrows and OK: no mouse, and nothing she has to learn or remember.

Her family’s half is an ordinary web app on their own phones and laptops, where they add the photographs, films and words. Both halves are served from one codebase.

Her screen

Built to be read from the sofa.

The screen never scrolls and is always dark. A header a fifth of the screen tall holds the channel number, a large clock and the date. Each photograph brings its own caption and date, a blurred copy of it fills the room behind, and the remote’s arrows go round the album without an end.

The date says only what the family wrote: a photograph dated August 1978 is never shown as 1 August 1978.

An instruction channel shows a routine one step at a time, and its arrows stop at the last step, because the end of a guide is the end of the task.

Objects and numbers

Every channel has a number, printed on a card.

The reader works like a keyboard: a card simply types its channel’s number, the same way the remote’s number keys do. A few numbers belong to her own screens: 00 shows every channel, 10 the people in her life, 98 brings her home.

A channel’s number is given when the channel is created and never changes, because a card in the house is printed with it. 99 asks who is at the keyboard, so a card or a stray key can never open the family’s workspace.

The family workspace

Where the family adds the memories.

The overview shows what her screen is showing right now, beside the channels and the missions still open.

A channel is created in three steps: the channel, the photographs, the details. An administrator chooses who can see it in the workspace: Private, Chosen or Everyone. Her own screen always shows everything.

A deleted channel waits in Recently deleted and can be put back; only an administrator can remove it for good, after a second confirmation.

  • AdministratorSees the whole archive, chooses who sees each memory, and manages the people, the screens and the settings.
  • Co-authorAdds memories, and sees the ones shared with everyone, the ones chosen for them, and their own.
  • The person herselfOpens her photographs without a PIN. Everyone else has a six-digit PIN, shown once and stored only as a hash.

Private by design

Only her screen and her family can read her archive.

  • The browser never talks to the database. Only Recall’s server holds the keys.
  • Every table is locked. Anyone not signed in is refused everything, and in production the server will not start if a lock is missing.
  • Photographs and films sit in private storage. They are streamed through the server; the browser never sees where they are kept.
  • Every upload is checked by what the file really is, not by its name or the type it claims, and stored under a new name.
  • Email addresses and phone numbers reach a signed-in administrator only, never her screen.

The result

A family platform with a home display: roles, private storage and an archive only the family can open.
